# Rhythm library sources

Researched 16 October 2026. Fretwise contains **51 strumming and 51 fingerpicking exercises across 16 styles**, with three or four variants per style on each page.

The linked teachers explain style, technique, and musical context. The app's grids are original one-bar practice arrangements, not claimed transcriptions of those lessons or specific recordings. A style has many valid accompaniments. Fingerpicking adaptations of funk, disco, and reggae teach the rhythmic texture using C major; they are not presented as the only traditional way to play those styles.

## Reading the arrangements

- A slot is an eighth note or a sixteenth note, as printed. Gaps mean no new attack, not an instruction to stop every ringing note.
- D/U are down/up strokes. B is a bass-only brush. X is a muted percussive stroke or tap; picking tabs mark it on the G row for visibility, without assigning a pitch.
- Picking numbers are string numbers in the source data; the rendered tab shows **frets** over open C major, x32010. Plus signs in data indicate simultaneous plucks. The thumb plays A/D, index G, middle B, and ring high e. A-to-D bass alternation over C is C-to-E (root to third), not root to fifth.
- 6/8 has two dotted-quarter pulses: count 1-2-3 / 4-5-6, with metronome clicks at 1 and 4. 3/4 has three quarter-note pulses, even if both bars contain six eighth notes.
- 12/8 shuffle examples use four dotted-quarter pulses and explicit triplet subdivisions. Playing the first and last subdivision gives a long-short feel. This is not a full 12-bar blues progression.
- Boston terminology varies across Vietnamese lessons. The app includes explicitly labeled 3/4 and 6/8 treatments.
- Bolero examples introduce bass/treble phrasing on a straight grid. They do not cover every traditional rhythmic inflection, bass run, or ornament. Bossa studies are one-bar preparations; longer phrases often span two bars.

External lessons need internet access. The counted exercises, references' titles/URLs, and practice notes are bundled with the offline PWA; external pages and videos are not cached.
